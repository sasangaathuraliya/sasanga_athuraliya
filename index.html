About me

Cars and motorsport have been my passion for as long as I can remember.
Now, as a Sri Lankan automotive engineer, I'm building a career around exactly that.

My name is Sasanga Athuraliya, an Automotive Engineering undergraduate soon to graduate from CINEC Campus, Malabe. I'm driven by a deep interest in chassis design, aerodynamics, and automotive design, and I'm working toward a career as an Aerodynamics Engineer or Automotive Design Engineer contributing to the kind of cutting-edge vehicle innovation that first got me hooked on this field.

I take an analytical, hands-on approach to engineering problems, with proficiency in SOLIDWORKS (CSWA and CSWP certified) and CATIA, backed by practical, real-world experience gained during my traineeship at Diesel & Motor Engineering PLC where I worked across commercial and passenger vehicle diagnostics, fault analysis, and service operations, including time as a Trainee Mercedes-Benz Service Advisor.

Leadership and community have shaped my journey as much as engineering has. I co-founded and served as the inaugural President of the Society of Automotive Engineering at CINEC Campus, building the first student-led automotive organisation on campus to bridge the gap between academia and industry. I've also been an active Engineering Undergraduate Representative for four years, liaising between students and faculty while organising and running campus events. Beyond the classroom, I volunteer to officiate at local motorsport events, and I'm a committed Rotaract volunteer, having led and contributed to multiple community service and cultural initiatives recognised at district and international levels.

I'm always excited to connect with automotive enthusiasts, engineers, and changemakers collaborating, sharing ideas, and pushing innovation forward across motorsport and volunteer communities alike.

This CAD project involved designing a custom transition air duct for an energy-optimized electric tray dryer for black pepper drying. Biomorphic Generative Design & Lattice-Integrated Formula Student Chassis

Individual Research Project | Topology Optimization & FEA Validation

The Challenge

Formula Student regulations demand lightweight yet structurally robust chassis designs. The challenge was to enhance the stiffness-to-weight ratio of a baseline spaceframe design through biomorphic topology optimization while maintaining FS-UK 2025 regulatory compliance and achieving a minimum 2.0 safety factor.

My Approach

Tools & Methodology:
• CATIA V6 for baseline 14-member steel spaceframe CAD with full FS rule validation
• Sectioned topology optimization (front, mid, rear sections) for targeted weight reduction
• Material comparison: AISI 4130 vs Ti-6Al-4V LPBF Post-HIP for optimal strength-to-weight
• Comprehensive FEA validation across 7 load cases (torsional stiffness, bending, von Mises stress, impact, buckling)
• 1:3 scale 3D-printed geometric validation model for design feasibility confirmation

Results & Outcomes

✓ Baseline CAD: Completed with zero FS-UK violations
✓ Methodology Validation: Research framework successfully established and documented
✓ FEA Validation: All 7 load cases passed with 2.0+ safety factor
✓ 3D Prototype: Printed scale model confirmed design feasibility
✓ Target Progress: Partial topology optimization results despite computational complexity

Key Skills Demonstrated

CAD Design (CATIA V6) • FEA Analysis • Topology Optimization • Research Methodology • Regulatory Compliance • Materials Selection • 3D Modeling & Simulation • Technical Problem-Solving
